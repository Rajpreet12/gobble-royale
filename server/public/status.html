What these numbers mean

p50, p95, p99

Sort every measurement from fastest to slowest. p50 is the one in the middle: half were faster. p95 is 95% of the way along: only the slowest 1 in 20 were worse. p99: only the slowest 1 in 100 were worse.

Why not the average? If 99 ticks take 1 ms and one takes 200 ms, the average is about 3 ms and looks healthy, but the p99 shows the 200 ms freeze that every player felt.

SLI, SLO, SLA and error budgets
  • SLI (indicator): what we measure, like p99 tick time.
  • SLO (objective): the target we hold ourselves to, like under 10 ms.
  • SLA (agreement): a promise to customers with a penalty if it's broken. A free game doesn't have one.
  • Error budget: how much failure the SLO allows. 99.5% uptime over 30 days allows 3.6 hours of downtime. Spend it on risky changes; if it runs out, stop and fix reliability.
How it's measured

The server times each tick and each request itself, and times each player's round trip by sending a ping every 2 seconds and timing the reply. The table uses the last 5 minutes; each chart point is one 30-second slice. Times are only recorded while someone is playing, so an idle server can't make the numbers look better than any player felt.

Raw numbers for monitoring tools are at /metrics in Prometheus format.
